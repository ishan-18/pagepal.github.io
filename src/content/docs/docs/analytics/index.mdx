The signals that make the mascot wince or look confused are the ones a product team wants to see in a dashboard: which buttons people rage-click, which fields fail validation, where they give up on a form, which endpoints fail. The **analytics plugin** records them and sends them where you choose.

```ts
import { createPal } from 'pagepal';
import { analytics } from 'pagepal/analytics';

const ux = analytics({ endpoint: '/api/ux' });
createPal({ watch: 'form#checkout', requests: true, plugins: [ux] });
```

That's the whole setup. Batches are POSTed to `/api/ux` with `navigator.sendBeacon`, which survives the page closing.

## What you get

<div class="not-content" style="margin: 1.5rem 0">
  <SummaryPreview summary={summary} caption="Sample data: a made-up week of checkout traffic, run through summarize()." />
</div>

- **Rage and dead clicks**, by element: the buttons that don't work, or don't look like they work.
- **Form errors** by field and reason (`patternMismatch` on the zip code), **submits**, and **abandonment**, with the field people were on when they left.
- **Failing and slow requests**, grouped by method and sanitized URL, and time spent waiting per page view.
- **Offline time**, and **how people feel about the mascot**: pokes and dismissals.

## Principles

- **Yours.** There is no pagepal server. Nothing is sent unless you configure an `endpoint` or a `send` function.
- **Private by default.** No typed values, masked labels, sanitized URLs, no identifiers that link page views, and it switches off for Do Not Track and Global Privacy Control. [Details →](/docs/analytics/privacy/)
- **Plays well with your stack.** Send to your own API, or to GA4, PostHog, Segment or Plausible. [Delivery →](/docs/analytics/delivery/)
- **Small.** About 3.5 KB gzipped, and only in bundles that import it.
