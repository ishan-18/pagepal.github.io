**pagepal** is a small mascot that sits in a corner of your page and reacts to what is actually happening on it. It winces at a form error and looks at the field that caused it. It waits while requests are in flight, covers its eyes while someone types a password, and looks confused when a button does nothing.

Most mascots are decoration. pagepal gives users feedback, and gives you data: the signals that move the mascot also power [first-party UX analytics](/docs/analytics/), such as rage clicks, dead clicks, form errors and abandonment, and failing endpoints, sent only where you choose.

## How it works

pagepal is built from three small ideas:

1. **Watchers** notice things on the page: a form's `invalid` event, a `fetch` in flight, the browser going offline, a click that changes nothing.
2. Each watcher raises a **signal**, a mood with a priority and sometimes a duration. When several signals are active at once, the [highest priority wins](/docs/guides/moods/), and the pal falls back when it ends.
3. The pal also emits **events** (`formError`, `request`, `frustration`, …) that you can listen to, or hand to the [analytics plugin](/docs/analytics/).

Everything renders in a Shadow DOM, so your CSS can't break it and its CSS can't leak into your page. It has no dependencies.
